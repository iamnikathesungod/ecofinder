      Animals only; plants and fungi are filtered out. iNaturalist shows research-grade species counts. Observation.org and Earthdeck are read through GBIF, the open database both publish to: Observation.org is its own GBIF dataset, and Earthdeck's maps are built from GBIF records, so that source covers all GBIF datasets. Because GBIF also carries iNaturalist and Observation.org records, the sources overlap and counts should be read per source, not added up. GBIF counts come from a sample of up to 300 photographed records. Each species links to National Geographic and Earthdeck searches; neither offers a public data feed. Place search by OpenStreetMap Nominatim.

      OBIS lists ocean species by number of records (animals only). The Animal Telemetry Network count is the number of tagged-animal deployments with a tracked fix inside the box; its public data cover older US satellite tags, so many places will have none. NOAA's National Ocean Service has no wildlife lookup of its own. Its marine science programs (coastal science centers, marine sanctuaries, and the Integrated Ocean Observing System that runs the telemetry network) publish into OBIS-USA, the US national node of OBIS, which is what the "NOAA & US federal marine data" source reads. That node also holds data from other agencies such as USGS and NMFS, so it is not NOS-only, and it is mostly US waters. Each species also links to the Ocean Service website. Two sources have no public data feed, so they are links rather than live results: Wildlife Insights (camera-trap data, free account needed to download) and Map of Life Regions (expected and recorded species for any area you draw).
